1. What Is an ATS and How Does It Work?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software used by employers to collect, parse, and rank job applications. Think of it as a digital gatekeeper standing between your resume and a recruiter's inbox.

Popular ATS Platforms in 2026

The most widely used ATS platforms include Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, Taleo (Oracle), BambooHR, and Jobvite. Each has slightly different parsing rules, but they all share the same core weakness: they struggle with complex formatting.

💡 The Core Insight

ATS systems are not as smart as you think. They parse your resume into fields (Name, Skills, Experience, Education) and match those fields to job requirements. Anything that interferes with that parsing — fancy layouts, columns, tables, images — gets you rejected instantly.

3. Resume Structure: Section by Section

Here's the optimal order for an ATS-friendly resume in 2026. Every section plays a strategic role in both parsing and readability.

1

Contact Information

Place your full name at the top in a slightly larger font. Include your city/state (not full address), phone number, professional email, LinkedIn URL, and portfolio link if relevant. Never put contact info in the header/footer — ATS skips those.

2

Professional Summary (3–4 sentences)

A concise paragraph that front-loads your most valuable keywords. Mention your title, years of experience, top skills, and the value you bring. Avoid vague terms like "hardworking" or "passionate." This section is prime ATS real estate.

3

Skills Section

List your hard skills as a comma-separated list or grouped categories. This is the easiest way to inject exact keywords from the job description. Avoid soft skills here — save those for your bullets.

4

Work Experience (Reverse Chronological)

List jobs starting with the most recent. Use the format: Job Title → Company Name → Location → Dates. Write 3–6 bullet points per role starting with action verbs. Quantify results wherever possible: "Increased sales by 34%" beats "Responsible for sales."

5

Education

Degree, major, university, graduation year. If you graduated within the last 3 years, put education before experience. Include GPA only if above 3.5.

6

Optional Sections

Certifications, Projects, Publications, Languages, Volunteer Work. These add keyword richness and differentiate you from other candidates with identical titles.

5. How to Use Keywords to Pass ATS

Keywords are the single most important factor in whether your resume passes ATS. Here's exactly how to find and use them.

Step 1: Mine the Job Description

Copy the job description into a text document. Identify the words that appear most frequently, especially technical skills, tools, certifications, and job titles. These are your primary keywords.

Step 2: Match, Don't Stuff

Incorporate keywords naturally throughout your resume — in your summary, skills section, and bullet points. Keyword stuffing (listing the same term 10 times) is detected by modern ATS and flagged negatively.

Example: Software Engineering Keywords (2026)

Python JavaScript React AWS REST APIs Docker CI/CD Git SQL Agile Microservices TypeScript Node.js PostgreSQL Kubernetes

Larger = higher ATS frequency weight. Include these verbatim — don't abbreviate if the JD spells it out.

Step 3: Use Exact Phrasing

If the job description says "Project Management Professional (PMP)," don't write "PMP Certified." ATS may not connect the two. Mirror the exact phrasing from the job description whenever possible.

⚠️

Watch out: Adding white text on a white background (keyword stuffing hidden from human eyes but visible to ATS) is now detected and penalized by most modern ATS systems. Don't do it.

6. 7 ATS Mistakes That Get You Rejected Instantly

Even small formatting errors can tank your ATS score. Avoid these common — and costly — mistakes:

1

Using a Creative Resume Template

That beautiful two-column design with colorful headers you found on Pinterest? ATS cannot parse it. Stick to clean, simple layouts. Save the design work for your portfolio.

2

Putting Contact Info in the Header

Microsoft Word and Google Docs have built-in "Header" zones. Most ATS systems completely skip this area, meaning your name and email vanish. Always place contact details in the main body of the document.

3

Using Images or Logos

Your headshot, company logos, or skill-rating graphics are invisible to ATS. Worse, they sometimes corrupt parsing of surrounding text. Never include images on an ATS resume.

4

Using Non-Standard Section Names

Calling your experience section "My Journey" or "Where I've Been" confuses ATS parsers. Stick to: Work Experience, Professional Experience, Education, Skills, Certifications, Summary.

5

Sending Only a PDF (Sometimes)

While most modern ATS can read PDFs, some older systems (especially in enterprise companies) still prefer .docx. If the application says "PDF," use PDF. If unsure, .docx is safer. GetPerfectResume lets you export both instantly.

6

Using Tables for Your Layout

Tables look neat in Word but become chaos inside ATS. The system may parse table cells out of order, jumbling your job titles, dates, and company names beyond recognition.

7

One-Size-Fits-All Resume

Sending the same resume to every job is the biggest ATS mistake of all. Each application needs a tailored version with keywords from that specific job description. Yes, every single one. (This is why tools like GetPerfectResume exist.)

FAQ: ATS Resume Questions Answered

What is an ATS-friendly resume?

An ATS-friendly resume is formatted so that Applicant Tracking Systems can accurately parse and read your information. This means using standard fonts, clear section headings, no images or tables, and keywords that match the job description. The goal is to achieve a high match score so your resume reaches a human recruiter.

What is the best file format for ATS — PDF or Word?

Both can work, but .docx (Microsoft Word) is universally compatible with all ATS systems including older ones. Modern ATS platforms like Greenhouse and Lever handle PDF very well. When in doubt, submit .docx. GetPerfectResume lets you download both instantly.

How do I check if my resume is ATS-friendly?

The simplest test: copy your entire resume and paste it into Notepad or a plain text editor. If the result looks clean and logical — with all sections, bullet points, and dates intact — your resume will likely parse well in ATS. If it looks like scrambled text, fix your formatting. You can also use GetPerfectResume's free ATS scanner.

What ATS score do I need to get an interview?

Aim for at least 70–80% keyword match with the job description. At 80%+, you're in the competitive zone for most roles. GetPerfectResume is designed to hit 90%+ automatically by analyzing the job description and optimizing your resume in real time.

Can I use a two-column resume for ATS?

Simple two-column layouts can work with modern ATS, but complex ones with sidebar sections are risky. ATS may read columns left-to-right across both columns simultaneously, creating garbled output. Do I need a different resume for every job?

Yes. Every job description has different keywords and requirements. Sending the same generic resume to 50 jobs is why most people don't hear back.
